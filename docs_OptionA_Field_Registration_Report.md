# Option A — Robust Sports Field Registration for GSR
## PnLCalib per-frame calibration + BroadTrack temporal tracking + Broadcast2Pitch constraint design
### Feasibility report and Kaggle test protocol (SoccerNet-GSR)

## 1. Objective

Build and rigorously evaluate a field-registration module that is accurate per frame, temporally coherent, and robust to sparse markings and narrow zoomed views, and that plugs into a Game State Reconstruction (GSR) pipeline as its pitch-localization component. The evaluation target is the SoccerNet-GSR dataset (also called sn-gamestate; introduced at CVPRW 2024, built on sequences the GSR paper describes: the test set contains 49 sequences of 750 frames each, 30 seconds at 25 fps, from 3 games of the Swiss Football League, annotated with point correspondences along field markings and goal posts). A note on naming: there is no dataset officially called "GSR 2023" — the two relevant releases are SoccerNet-Calibration-2023 (single frames, used for calibration-only evaluation) and SoccerNet-GSR/sn-gamestate (video sequences, used for temporal and end-to-end GSR evaluation). This report uses both, each for what it is designed for.

## 2. System design

The system is a three-layer stack in which each layer is an existing, published, open-source component, modified only where the papers themselves justify the modification.

**Layer 1 — Detection front-end (NBJW/PnLCalib networks).** Two HRNet-w48 encoder–decoder networks produce 57+1 keypoint heatmaps and 23+1 line-extremity heatmaps (PnLCalib, Fig. 1). Pretrained SoccerNet weights are publicly released in the PnLCalib and NBJW repositories. This is the natural choice for compatibility: BroadTrack itself uses exactly this detector on sn-gamestate, keeping only keypoints that are actual marks, line intersections, or line/circle-arc intersections and discarding keypoints derived from other geometric cues (BroadTrack, Sec. 4.1). PnLCalib's hierarchical keypoint machinery (base set Kp, extremities Kpe, circle-derived sets Kp1/Kp2 via ellipse fitting and tangent-point geometry, additional set Kp3, plus disambiguation via reprojection error and cross-product sign consistency) then augments the raw detections to guarantee enough correspondences even when markings are sparse.

**Layer 2 — Per-frame calibration and refinement (PnLCalib, upgraded).** Initial calibration from keypoints, then the PnL refinement module minimizes the unified cost of Eq. (7): C = α Σ d(l_i, l_d,i) + (1−α) Σ d(x̄_d,j, x_j), with α = 0.6 set empirically in the paper, point-to-line distances per Eq. (5) and keypoint reprojection error per Eq. (6), solved as a nonlinear least-squares problem, with a projection-error threshold gating which detected lines enter the line set L. Two upgrades are imported from Broadcast2Pitch, described in Section 3.

**Layer 3 — Temporal tracking (BroadTrack).** The camera state κ = {f, k1, φ, θ, γ, Cx, Cy, Cz} (single focal length, one Brown–Conrady radial term k1, pan/tilt/roll, focal-point position) is updated each frame by Levenberg–Marquardt minimization of L = L_F + L_OF + ω·L_T: the field-marking reprojection term L_F with Cauchy robust loss, the optical-flow term L_OF (correspondences restricted to the field surface, player regions masked out via detections, back-projected to the Z = 0 plane), and the tripod constraint L_T = δ − ‖O* − T‖₂ that keeps the optical axis at fixed distance δ from the tripod rotation center T. Confidence is monitored online as the Jaccard index s between the binary mask of detected markings and the projected field template; when s < 0.5 the system reinitializes from the single-frame calibrator — which in our stack is Layer 2. The tripod center T and offset δ are estimated per game by first running the tracker without the tripod constraint and then optimizing T and δ from the resulting trajectory (BroadTrack, Sec. 4.1); this uses no ground-truth labels, so it introduces no evaluation leakage.

Justification for keeping every BroadTrack ingredient: in the paper's ablation on sn-gamestate (Table 3), including radial distortion k1 is the single largest contributor (JaC5 from 42.09 to 54.1), optical flow and the tripod constraint each add further gains (full model 56.88 JaC5, MedRE 2.37 px), and the paper argues their main benefit — smoothness and physically plausible focal-point localization (NBJW's per-frame focal point can travel up to 20 m within one sequence, Fig. 3) — is only partially captured by single-frame metrics. Overall, BroadTrack roughly halves the mean reprojection error and gains over 15% Jaccard versus prior art on SoccerNet, which is exactly the "coherence and robustness" the GSR task needs.

## 3. Constraint upgrades imported from Broadcast2Pitch

Broadcast2Pitch estimates a homography by minimizing the sum of squared residuals of three confidence-weighted terms (its Eqs. 2–6): algebraic line residuals r = w·(ℓ′ᵀp̃) over dense samples thresholded at τ > 0.8 from 18 line heatmaps, an algebraic conic residual r = w·(p̃ᵀC′p̃) for the 9.15 m center circle with C′ = GᵀCG, and a keypoint fallback with confidence-weighted reprojection residuals, initialized by DLT when at least four keypoints are available and refined with Levenberg–Marquardt. Two of these ideas transfer cleanly into Layer 2; the third requires a deliberate mathematical fix.

**Upgrade 1 — Confidence weighting (low risk, adopt as-is).** PnLCalib's cost treats every detected point and line equally. Broadcast2Pitch weights every residual by its heatmap confidence. We multiply each term in PnLCalib's Eq. (7) by the corresponding peak heatmap value. This changes only the relative influence of observations, not the geometry, and degrades gracefully to the original cost when confidences are uniform.

**Upgrade 2 — Explicit conic term (the substantive change).** PnLCalib already exploits the circles indirectly — ellipse fitting produces the Kp1/Kp2 keypoint sets, and the paper's appendix derives the conic tangent-point geometry (x̄ᵀc̄x̄ = 0, p̄ᵀc̄x̄ = 0, with world-coordinate reprojection c = H⁻ᵀc̄H⁻¹) — but the optimizer only ever sees those circles as a handful of derived points. We add a direct conic residual over all circle-arc evidence. The critical reconciliation: Broadcast2Pitch's conic residual is algebraic, while PnLCalib's cost is built from geometric pixel distances (point-to-line and reprojection distances, which the paper deliberately makes unit-comparable by representing lines through their endpoints). Summing raw algebraic conic values with pixel distances would produce an unbalanced, scale-dependent objective. The fix is standard: replace the algebraic residual with its first-order geometric approximation, the Sampson distance d_S(p̃) = (p̃ᵀC′p̃) / ‖∇(p̃ᵀC′p̃)‖, which is expressed in pixels and therefore commensurate with the other terms. The upgraded cost becomes C = α Σ w·d_line + β Σ w·d_S,circle + (1−α−β) Σ c·d_kpt, with α and β tuned once on the validation split only (PnLCalib provides α = 0.6 as the starting point for the point/line balance).

Where the circle evidence comes from is a design decision that must be stated honestly. The NBJW/PnLCalib detector outputs line extremities and circle-derived keypoints, not the dense center-circle heatmap that Broadcast2Pitch samples. Two implementation routes exist. Route A (no retraining): feed the conic term with the ellipse fitted during PnLCalib's Kp1/Kp2 construction, sampling points along the fitted arc and weighting by the fit's support; cheap, but the conic term is then only as good as the ellipse fit. Route B (light retraining): add a circle-heatmap output head to the line-extremity network — SoccerNet annotations already contain the circle point chains PnLCalib's training-data pipeline uses for ellipse fitting, so training targets exist without new labeling. The test protocol below evaluates Route A first because it preserves the "no new training" property; Route B is the fallback if the ablation shows the conic term is starved of samples.

**Upgrade 3 — Keypoint fallback.** Broadcast2Pitch falls back to weighted keypoint correspondences when lines are sparse. PnLCalib already implements a stronger version of this (hierarchical sets, missing-keypoint retrieval, grid-search disambiguation), so nothing structural is added; the fallback behavior is inherited and merely becomes confidence-weighted via Upgrade 1.

**Interface between Layers 2 and 3.** Two mismatches must be handled. First, BroadTrack's L_F term as published extracts marking points by mean shift from semantic segmentation maps; with our detector front-end, the marking correspondences instead come from the detected keypoints and line extremities, which is precisely the substitution BroadTrack itself performed for its sn-gamestate experiments. Second, PnLCalib's camera model is a distortion-free pinhole while BroadTrack's includes k1; at each reinitialization, Layer 2 provides {f, R, C} and k1 is carried over from the last tracked estimate (or initialized to 0 for the very first frame), after which the LM update re-optimizes the full κ including k1. This is consistent with BroadTrack's own reinitialization design, which restarts from a single-frame calibrator that does not model distortion. **[to verify at implementation time]** the exact reinitialization entry point in the released BroadTrack code, so that the PnLCalib estimate can be injected without modifying the tracker's internal state machine.

## 4. Known limitations, stated up front

This system is a main-broadcast-camera tracker. The tripod model assumes an elevated tripod-mounted camera; PnLCalib itself reports that calibration fails on fisheye shots from inside goals and on close-ups with few or no visible landmarks (its Fig. 6), so on the multi-view portions of SoccerNet the completeness rate will reflect deliberate abstention rather than silent wrong answers — which is the correct behavior for GSR, where a wrong homography poisons every player position in the frame. BroadTrack also reports a residual "vertigo effect": high-frequency focal-length variation because position uncertainty can be traded against focal length at long distances; we measure but do not attempt to fix this. Finally, the GSR baseline's own smoothing (Savitzky–Golay, ±2° / ±2 m correction range, 15-frame look-ahead delay) is kept only as a comparison baseline, not as a component: the tracking layer is expected to make it redundant, and the ablation tests exactly that claim.

## 5. Rigorous test protocol on Kaggle

**Data.** SoccerNet-GSR is downloaded via the official `SoccerNet` pip package (the sn-gamestate repository documents the procedure; access requires the free NDA password from soccer-net.org, which on Kaggle must be stored as a Kaggle Secret, never hardcoded). Because Kaggle sessions are time-limited and the dataset is large video data, the recommended setup is a one-time download session that stages the data as a private Kaggle Dataset, which subsequent runs attach read-only; frames are decoded per sequence on the fly rather than extracted wholesale to disk. SoccerNet-Calibration-2023 is staged the same way for the calibration-only experiments. Model weights (NBJW/PnLCalib releases) are small and attached as a second private dataset so runs are fully offline-reproducible.

**Compute plan.** All heavy neural inference (the two HRNet-w48 forward passes per frame) is run once per sequence and cached to disk as heatmap peak lists (keypoints, extremities, confidences) — a few kilobytes per frame. Every optimization-layer experiment and every ablation then reruns from the cache on CPU, which makes the full ablation grid feasible within Kaggle's weekly GPU quota and prevents detector nondeterminism from contaminating comparisons: all methods in every table consume byte-identical detections. Each sequence writes an independent result file so an interrupted session resumes without recomputation.

**Metrics.** Per-frame accuracy: JaC at 5 and 10 px on the full-HD sn-gamestate frames plus mean and median reprojection error and completeness rate, exactly the BroadTrack Section 4.1 protocol, so our numbers are directly comparable to its published Table 3 row (56.88 / 79.79 / MRE 5.02 / MedRE 2.37 on the same test set). Calibration-challenge comparability: JaC5 on SoccerNet-Calibration-2023 at 960×540, comparable to the published reinitialization table (TVCalib 52.9, NBJW 73.7, BroadTrack reinit 75.25). Temporal coherence, which single-frame metrics only partially reflect (BroadTrack's own argument): per-sequence standard deviation of frame-to-frame deltas in pan, tilt, and focal length, and total focal-point travel distance per sequence (the paper's Fig. 3 diagnostic, where NBJW travels up to 20 m). Downstream GSR quality: GS-HOTA, the official SoccerNet-GSR metric, obtained by swapping our module into the TrackLab-based sn-gamestate baseline as its calibration component, keeping every other module frozen so that any GS-HOTA delta is attributable to registration alone.

**Baselines and ablations.** Four external baselines, all on identical cached detections where applicable: raw NBJW (no refinement), PnLCalib single-frame (published configuration, α = 0.6), BroadTrack as published, and the GSR-paper approach of per-frame prediction plus Savitzky–Golay smoothing. The ablation grid then adds our components one at a time in this order: confidence weighting; conic term (Route A); temporal layer without tripod and without optical flow; +k1; +optical flow; +tripod; full system — mirroring BroadTrack's Table 3 structure so each increment is attributable. Statistical discipline: results reported as per-sequence means with medians alongside (reprojection errors are heavy-tailed, hence BroadTrack reports both MRE and MedRE); fixed random seeds; α and β tuned exclusively on the validation split with the test set touched once, at the end; failure cases (s < 0.5 stretches, reinitialization counts and durations) logged and reported, since BroadTrack treats reinitialization frequency as a first-class robustness indicator (60 reinitializations averaging 4 frames over 60,000 frames in its long-form experiment).

**Acceptance criteria.** The system is considered successful for GSR integration if, versus the strongest baseline on sn-gamestate: JaC5 and MedRE are at least matched, the temporal-jitter diagnostics improve, completeness rate does not collapse (abstention is acceptable, but must remain in the same regime as BroadTrack's reported CR behavior), and GS-HOTA improves when only the registration module is swapped. If the conic term (Route A) shows no measurable gain in the ablation, it is dropped or replaced by Route B rather than kept for its own sake — the goal is performance and robustness, not architectural completeness.

## 6. Milestones

Phase 1: reproduce PnLCalib and BroadTrack numbers on the staged Kaggle data with released weights — this validates the entire data and metric plumbing before any modification exists. Phase 2: implement confidence weighting and the Sampson-normalized conic term inside PnLCalib's refinement; ablate on the validation split. Phase 3: wire the upgraded PnLCalib as BroadTrack's reinitializer; run the full ablation grid on the sn-gamestate test set. Phase 4: swap into the TrackLab GSR baseline and measure GS-HOTA. Phase 5: failure analysis (per-sequence worst cases, reinitialization logs, vertigo-effect measurement) and final report tables.

## 7. Risk register

Runtime per frame is dominated by LM iterations, not the network, once detections are cached; if optimization is slow, the mitigation is warm-starting from κ_{t−1} (which BroadTrack already does) and capping iterations. The conic term can be degenerate when only a short arc is visible; mitigation is gating the term on minimum arc support, exactly as PnLCalib gates lines on a projection-error threshold. Annotation bias: BroadTrack found that homography-based annotations penalize models with radial distortion, which motivated its choice of the point-correspondence annotations in sn-gamestate — our protocol follows that choice, so this known bias is avoided. Kaggle session limits are handled by the per-sequence checkpointing described above. ## 8. Conclusion

Option A is feasible with moderate engineering effort and no mandatory retraining. Its three layers are individually published, benchmarked, and open-source, and they already interoperate (BroadTrack consumes the NBJW detector; PnLCalib extends it). The only genuinely novel technical work is the Sampson-normalized conic term and the confidence weighting inside PnLCalib's refinement — both small, both ablatable, both reversible. The Kaggle protocol above tests every added component against the strongest published baselines on the exact datasets and metrics those baselines reported, which is the standard required for a claim of "best performance, coherence, and robustness" to be defensible.
